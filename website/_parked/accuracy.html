JumpLab uses a single 2D phone camera, not a lab system. It is built for consistent, repeatable field testing, and it reports its own uncertainty instead of hiding it.

Known limitations

  • 2D only. Motion toward or away from the camera isn't measured, so jumps must be filmed side-on.
  • Hidden far side. The arm and leg away from the camera are partly hidden; their positions are estimated.
  • Foot markers drift. Tracked heel and toe points move 1–4 cm while a foot is planted, so takeoff and landing are timed to about ±2 frames.
  • One-frame resolution. Every time is ±1 frame: ±8 ms at 120 fps, ±33 ms at 30 fps.
  • Calibration matters. Metres depend on a reference in the jump plane; a reference closer to the camera makes every distance read short.
  • Average body model. The centre of mass uses typical adult segment proportions, not the individual athlete's.
