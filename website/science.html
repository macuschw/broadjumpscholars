Science

Physics you can check by hand.

No black box. Every number comes from five plain steps, each built on physics taught in high school and college.

  1. 1

    Pose tracking

    An AI model finds 33 body landmarks in every frame of the video.

    pi(t) = (xi, yi)

    Pixel positions, every frame

  2. 2

    Smoothing

    Gaps are filled and jitter removed with a low-pass filter run forwards and backwards, so nothing shifts in time.

    fc ≈ 10 Hz

    Zero-lag Butterworth filter

  3. 3

    Parabola fit

    In the air only gravity acts, so the centre of mass follows a parabola. Fitting it gives the takeoff velocity.

    x(t) = x0 + vxt
    y(t) = y0 + vyt − ½gt2

    θ = atan2(vy, vx), h = vy2/2g

  4. 4

    Vector geometry

    Body angles come from the line between two landmarks, measured from vertical. The centre of mass is a weighted average of 14 body segments.

    α = atan2(Δx, Δy)

    rCoM = Σmiri/Σmi

  5. 5

    Frame counting

    Takeoff and landing are found from the toe and heel landmarks. Times come from counting frames between them.

    T = (fland − ftakeoff) / fps

    1 frame = 8.3 ms at 120 fps

Pixels to metres

A tape measure filmed along the jump line sets the scale:

scale = known distance ÷ pixel distance

If the tape is nearer to or farther from the camera than the jump, the scale is corrected by the ratio of those distances, because something twice as far away looks half as big:

scalejump = scaleref × djump ÷ dref

Gravity is the built-in check: fitted freely, the parabola should give g ≈ 9.81 m/s².

Why it's done this way

Why fit a curve instead of timing one frame?

Each tracked position is off by a few millimetres. At 120 fps, 5 mm of noise between two frames swings a frame-to-frame speed by about ±0.8 m/s. A parabola through 50 frames averages that out, and physics says the path has to be a parabola, so the fit can't wander.

Why the centre of mass, not the hips or hands?

In the air, gravity is the only force on the body as a whole, so only its centre of mass follows a true parabola; arms and legs swing around it. JumpLab estimates the centre of mass from 14 body segments with standard mass fractions. This demo uses the hip as a stand-in.

Why film in slow motion?

Times are counted in frames. At 30 fps each frame is 33 ms, so a 0.43 s flight could read anywhere from 0.40 to 0.47 s; at 240 fps the step is 4 ms. Slow motion barely changes distances or angles. It is what makes the timing meaningful.
