FAQ

Are the numbers on this site real?

The sample jump and the figures on these pages are simulated, and labeled as demo data. Your own jumps are real: a video analyzed on the Analyze page, a jump measured on the Live page, or results opened from the JumpLab app all go through analysis that was checked against tape-measured jumps.

What's the difference between Upload and Live?

Upload analyzes a video you've already filmed: the distance plus takeoff speed and angle, body angles, contact and flight times, the tracked skeleton and charts. Live uses the phone's camera in the browser and says each distance out loud the moment the jumper holds the landing, with nothing to upload. When you stop, Live can send its recording for the full analysis, so you get both.

What equipment do I need?

A phone, a tripod, and a tape measure on the floor for the scale. For uploads, slow motion (120–240 fps) makes the timing numbers meaningful. Live works with any recent phone in its web browser. A laptop or second phone as a remote screen is optional. See the setup guide.

How accurate is it?

In our tape checks, jumps that land with the feet together came within about 0.3 in (1 cm) of the tape. With the feet apart, one side-on camera can't see the far foot, and the reading was off by 1.5–5 in, so the site warns you when it sees this. Live's quick estimate is usually within about 1% of the full analysis of the same jump. Setup matters most: tape on the camera's side of the feet, the camera square to the jump, and toes level with the start of the tape.

Can I use normal 30 fps video?

Yes for distance and angles, but timing is only good to ±33 ms per event. Slow motion (120–240 fps) makes contact and flight times meaningful. Live runs at the 30–60 fps a phone's browser gives, which is fine for the distance.

Does it know when a jump doesn't count?

Yes. A step forward or back after landing, a hand put down, or a fall marks the jump as not counting, and the distance is crossed out instead of shown as a result. In Live, the reason is said out loud. These rules were tuned on our own test jumps, so tell us if one gets it wrong.

What else does it check?

Anything that makes a number unreliable: a blurry video, someone else walking through the shot, feet apart at takeoff or landing, or the body leaving the frame. Serious problems show in red above the results, and the numbers they affect are marked "Not reliable", with how to fix it next time.

Can one video have several jumps?

Yes. Every jump in an uploaded video is found and measured on its own, even with two people taking turns. You get a list with each jump's distance, which ones count, and the best one.

What is the remote screen?

A laptop or second phone that shows the distances in big type, says them out loud, and runs the phone on the tripod: set the scale, stop, measure again, so nobody has to touch the filming phone. Open it on the Live page and scan its QR code with the filming phone. No accounts or sign-in. Afterwards it can bring the video and the results over.

Does my video get uploaded?

Only when you upload a video, or choose "Get the full data" after measuring live: it is sent to the JumpLab analysis server, analyzed, and deleted straight away. Live tracks on the phone itself and uploads nothing while measuring; if a remote screen asks for the recording afterwards, it passes through the server and is deleted as soon as the remote screen has saved or analyzed it (within an hour at most). Nothing is kept on the server. Opening a results file from the JumpLab app never uploads anything.

Where are my results saved?

Only in your own browser, under “My jumps” on the Analyze page, so you can open them again on a later visit. They never leave your device and nobody else can see them; another browser or device starts empty, and clearing your browsing data deletes them. Videos aren't saved, so add one back to see it under the skeleton. To keep copies anywhere, use “Download results”, or after measuring live “Save the video” and “Save the results” (a spreadsheet of every jump).

Can I see feet and inches?

Yes: switch between m and ft at the top of any page (in the menu on a phone), or on the Live screen. Every distance and speed changes, and the choice is remembered.
